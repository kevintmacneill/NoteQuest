Music basics

Note values

Whole = 4 quarter beats, half = 2, quarter = 1, eighth = ½, sixteenth = ¼. A dot adds half the original value.

The time signature and tempo tell you how these durations fit the music.

Intervals

Count letter names inclusively to find the interval number: C–D is a second; C–E is a third; C–G is a fifth. Quality tells whether it is major, minor, perfect, augmented or diminished.

Reading the staff

A five-line staff represents pitches using lines and spaces. Treble clef fixes G4 on its second line; bass clef fixes F3 on its fourth line. Ledger lines extend the staff.

Chords in a major key

For a major scale, the diatonic triad pattern is I major, ii minor, iii minor, IV major, V major, vi minor and vii° diminished.

Building a major scale

Follow whole (W) and half (H) steps: W–W–H–W–W–W–H. Example: C D E F G A B, returning to C.

Practice gently

Use a comfortable bench and relaxed arms. Work slowly enough to be accurate, pause when needed, then increase the tempo gradually. Pain is a reason to stop.
